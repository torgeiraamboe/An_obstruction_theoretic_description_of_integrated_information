\section{Introduction}

Integrated Information Theory (IIT) is a mathematical description of the existing consciousness of a system, developed mainly by Tononi and his collaborators, \citeme. The theory is centered around the computation of $\Phi$, which is a measure of the \emph{integrated information} in the system. Informally, a system $S$ in a state $s$, has integrated information, captured by $\Phi(S,s)>0$, if the information generated by the entire system's cause-effect structure in state $s$ is greater than the information generated by the combined cause-effect structure of partitions of the system. In other words, it is a measurable  emergent property of the system. The formal mathematical framework for computing $\Phi$ is rigorous and technical, and uses detailed features of probability theory and measure theory, see \citeme.

Classical integrated information is defined through an optimization over partitions and therefore lacks the functorial behavior expected of a categorical invariant. In particular, it is not functorial with respect to subsystem inclusions \citeme. Motivated by the work of Tull and Kleiner on a categorical version of IIT \citeme, we introduce in the present paper a cohomological formulation of integration, interpreting integration as the obstruction to assembling compatible local causal factorizations into a global factorization. These obstructions live in mathematical objects called \emph{cohomology groups}, which are abstract formulations of the amount of holes a system has in different dimensions. Obstruction theory was originally developed by Stiefel and Whitney to prove the non-existence of certain vector-fields on manifolds \citeme, but has later been applied in a plethora of mathematical contexts \citeme. 

More specifically, given a physical system $S$ in a state $s$, we assign to every subsystem its local factorization data, organized into a factorization presheaf $\F$. The associated \v{C}ech cohomology $H^*(S;\F)$ measures whether these local factorizations can be assembled into a compatible global factorization.Intuitively, we wish to build a mathematical structure whose $n$-dimensional holes contain all the relevant information about $n$-th order integration. We then define natural first degree obstruction classes $[c^1]$, which we show implies properties for IIT. In particular, if there is a non-trivial obstruction class, then the system has integrated information. These classes detect whether local causal factorizations can be assembled into a compatible global factorization. In this sense, integrated information is interpreted as the failure of descent for causal factorizations.

\begin{theorem}
    Let $s$ be a state of a system $S$. If there is a non-zero obstruction class $[c^1] \in H^1(S;\F)$, then $\Phi(S,s)>0$. 
\end{theorem}

The upshot of this work is that one can study integrated information from a new lens, and apply fundamental ideas from algebraic topology and homological algebra to study phenomena in consciousness. In particular, our theory gives a much more general approach to integrated information, that abstracts away many of the optimization-specific features of IIT while retaining its notion of reducibility. We expect that our methods can be combined with topological data analysis, see \citeme for an introduction, to locate minimum partitions (MIP) and connect integrated information to new developments in the relationship between topology and information theory, see for example \citeme and \citeme.

In the last section we consider a natural extension of our framework: higher order integration. In particular, our framework naturally allows us to introduce higher order obstruction classes $[c^k]$ that detect higher order obstructions to gluing. The above theorem proves that 
\[\Phi = 0  \implies [c^1] = 0,\]
but we conjecture that the converse is not true due to the possible non-vanishing of the higher order obstruction classes.  

We make no claim surrounding the validity of IIT to explain consciousness and the properties of experience, and make no contributions to the ongoing debates \citeme. We only wish to connect some of IIT's mathematical properties to other mathematical concepts known to measure similar features. We view this work not as a new standard for IIT, but as a starting point to explore some of the rich mathematical structures related to it.
